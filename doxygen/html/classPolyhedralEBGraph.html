Detailed Description

The cut cells of one level of the polyhedral geometry, and what they connect to.

Holds, over the level's cut tiles, the classification of every cell, the surface every cut cell is reconstructed from, and for every face of every cell what lies across it: nothing, a cell of this level, a cell of the finer level, a cell of the coarser level, or the domain boundary. The surface is the source of everything else about a cut cell – its polyhedron and moments are rebuilt from it on demand – and it regrids: a coarse surface's corners are fine nodes and its crossings are the children's. A cell whose face lies across a level boundary, or opens onto a covered cell, is not described by its own surface alone: PolyhedralGeometryShop stitches it once, from the stored cells of the level across, writes the crossings it takes from there into the surface and keeps the faces it changes as face overrides, so that the cell's body is rebuilt from its own surface and its own overrides with no call to the implicit function.

The graph covers the cut tiles only. A cell outside every tile is regular or covered, never cut, and which of the two is the geometry's box classification rather than the graph's business.

Built in two steps: define makes one level from its implicit function and its tiles, and knows about the domain boundary and about faces that open onto no tile of its own level; link, given two adjacent levels, marks the coarse cells the fine level carries and the coarse faces that open onto them.

Build the graph of one level over its cut tiles.

Load balances the tiles by cell count, reconstructs every cell of every tile and its ghost region from the implicit function, classifies it as the polyhedral generator does, keeping every cell that holds anything – a body with nothing in it is a regular cell, and nothing else is discarded, since a cell's interface is what closes the surface against its neighbours – keeps the surface of every cell whose corners disagree, cut or not, and of every cell beside one the finer level carries, and sets the face states this level can know by itself: closed or open from the apertures, open onto the domain boundary, and open onto a cell no tile of this level carries. Collective.

Build the graph of a level over a given layout, from another graph of the same level.

The other graph's cell states, face states and surfaces are copied onto the new layout's valid cells, and exchanged into its ghost cells; the ghost cells no tile of the new layout carries are classified from the implicit function by their corners, as define classifies them. The new layout must cover the same cells as the other graph's, since the surfaces come from it alone; a layout of another cell count stops the run. The refined mask is not copied: it is the finer level's to set, so link the copy to its finer level afterwards as the original was. Nor are the face overrides, which are stitched afresh on the copy. Collective.

Find the cells of a region that one body cannot describe, or that are mostly a gap of fluid.

A cell the surface enters as more than one sheet holds more than one piece of fluid or more than one piece of solid. Two pieces of fluid – a plate between them – are more than one body can hold, and the cell is filled, which is the only reading that stays single valued and keeps a barrier a barrier instead of letting the fluid run through it. One piece of fluid with several pieces of solid is either a gap of fluid thinner than the cell between two solid lobes, or pieces that merely sit apart in it, such as two solid tips at opposite corners of a face. The two share their topology and their corners, and a pattern that repeats at every scale – a surface lying in a node plane – looks the same in every subcell as well, so it is the volume that decides: the cell's body is built from its crossings, and the cell is filled when the fluid is the smaller part of it, erring toward blocking a gap, and kept, as one body with several sheets of interface, when the fluid fills most of it. Fluid pieces are counted the way the body joins them: across a face by its face decision, never through the interior. Filling is a classification and changes no value another level reads, so the children a coarse cell restricts against still agree with it about where every edge is crossed – which is what a snap of the nodes themselves cannot promise.

A cell the finer level carries is left alone: the feature is resolved up there, and this level's reading of it is not the one that gets used. Only the combinatorics are read, so no root is solved for here.

Nothing is counted here. The region a caller asks about reaches past the cells it owns, so that a cell can see whether its neighbours were filled, and those reaches overlap between neighbouring boxes; a count taken here would report a cell near a box boundary once for every box that reaches it. The caller counts over the cells it owns.

Parameters
[in]a_functionImplicit function, read at face centres and along the edges of multi-sheet cells.
[in]a_nodeValuesNode values of the region.
[in]a_regionCells to ask.
[in]a_coveredCells a finer level carries.
[in]a_probLoLower-left corner of the domain.

Mark, on the coarse level, the faces that open onto cells the fine level carries.

The fine tiles are coarsened onto the coarse layout the way Realm builds its valid-cell mask: a marker on the coarsened fine layout is copied onto the coarse layout and its ghost cells. An open face of an unrefined coarse cell onto a cell the marker reaches is described by the fine level. The coarse level already knows which of its cells are refined – it was told when it was defined – so the marker is checked against that rather than written over it, and a disagreement between the coarse level's covered region and the fine level's tiles stops the run. The fine level's own faces onto the coarse level were set when it was defined. Collective.

Parameters
[in,out]a_coarseGraph of the coarser level.
[in]a_fineGraph of the finer level, a factor-two refinement of it.

◆ spreadAnchors()

long long PolyhedralEBGraph::spreadAnchors ( )

Spread anchoring through the open faces of this level until it stops.

Filling a cell closes the faces onto it, and fluid that reached the rest of the fluid only through such faces is left sealed off: beside a filled gap thinner than a cell, the slivers at the gap's corners are joined to one another and to nothing else. No stencil fits in fluid that holds no regular cell, so such fluid is filled. A cut cell is anchored when an open face joins it to an anchored cell; the spread runs box by box with an exchange in between until no box anchors anything new. A face on the domain boundary anchors nothing, and a cell the finer level carries passes nothing through itself: what it holds is the finer level's. Collective.

Returns
Number of cells anchored, over all ranks.
