Detailed Description

The faces of a box's cut cells that do not come from the cells' own chords.

A cut cell's body is built from its surface, and most of its faces are exactly what the surface's chords give. Two kinds of face are not. A face onto a cell the finer level carries is described at the finer level's resolution: the chord is replaced by the finer cells' chords, merged into the polygons stored here, less any quadrant across which the finer level filled a cell. A face onto a cell this level filled is closed, and what it gave up becomes interface in the plane of that face; such a face stores no polygon, only that it is closed. With these the body of every cut cell is a function of its own surface and its own entries here, and needs nothing from its neighbours or from the finer level.

In two dimensions a face is a segment, and the finer level's description of it is its crossing, which the surface already carries. What is left to store is which half of a face on a refinement boundary is closed, where the finer cell across it was filled, and which faces are closed onto a filled cell; no polygon is stored.

Stored compressed, in the order the faces are applied: the cells in increasing order, each with its faces, each face with its polygons, each polygon with its vertices. A cell's restricted faces come before its closed ones, and each kind in increasing face order, which is the order a body applies them in. Cells are ordered as a BoxIterator meets them, the highest direction the most significant, and found by bisection in that order.
