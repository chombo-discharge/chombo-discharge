Detailed Description

Helmholtz operator alpha*a*phi + beta*div(b*grad(phi)), assembled as a PETSc matrix over the rows of a PetscGrid.

Unlike EBHelmholtzOp, which applies the operator to data, this class assembles the operator once as a matrix so that it can be solved with PETSc. The domain boundary conditions enter through PetscHelmholtzDomainBC, which gives the flux through a domain face as w_B * g + sum_i w_i * phi_i with g the boundary data. The w_i go into the matrix. The w_B are kept per domain face when the matrix is assembled, and w_B * g goes into the right-hand side, see addBoundaryConditionsToRHS. The boundary data can thus change without the matrix being reassembled.

Supported so far: a single level, no embedded boundaries, no periodic directions. Anything else aborts.

The operator is assembled finite-volume style. The row of a cell is its finite-volume balance divided by dx^SpaceDim and kappa-weighted, as in FieldSolverGMG: kappa*alpha*a*phi plus beta/dx times the sum, over the faces of the cell (including the EB face), of the face area fraction (the boundary area on the EB face) times b times s*F. Here F is the face flux, the derivative of phi in the +dir direction on the face, which is a stencil over PETSc rows, and s = -1 on the low side of the cell and +1 on the high side. The right-hand side must use the same weighting: a volume source is multiplied by kappa, and every factor that multiplies the stencil part of a face flux in the matrix also multiplies its boundary data part w_B * g on the right-hand side, see addBoundaryConditionsToRHS. Without embedded boundaries kappa and the area fractions are 1, and are not applied yet. Cells covered by a finer level have no row. Multilevel assembly is not implemented yet; it follows this scheme:

  • Face between two valid cells on the same level: the two-point flux, as on a single level.
  • Domain face: the stencils and the boundary weight w_B from PetscHelmholtzDomainBC::getFaceFluxStencil. The stencil has parts on the next coarser level, this level, and the next finer level. The part on this level may contain coarse-fine ghost cells, which are replaced as on the fine side of a refinement boundary below. Any other entry that is not a PETSc row, e.g. a cell covered by a finer grid, aborts, as do entries outside the ghost cells of the PetscGrid maps.
  • Fine side of a refinement boundary: the two-point flux between the fine cell and the ghost cell across the face. The ghost cell is not a PETSc row, so it is replaced by its interpolation stencil from EBMultigridInterpolator::getGhostStencil, which is over cells on the fine level and the next coarser level, and mapped with PetscGrid::getPetscStencilWithCoarserLevel.
  • Coarse side of a refinement boundary: the flux through the coarse face is the area-weighted average of the fine face fluxes that cover it, using the same fine-face stencils as the fine rows. The coarse and fine rows thus see the same flux through the interface, which makes the composite operator conservative.

The coefficients alpha, beta, a and b are multiplied into the matrix entries, so changing any of them means refilling the matrix. The a- and b-coefficients are held by reference, so the caller can change their values in place; setCoefficients points the operator to other data, and setAlphaAndBeta changes alpha and beta. Neither touches the matrix: fillMatrix is the only function that does. It refills the existing matrix, keeping its preallocation and sparsity pattern, so the AMG preconditioner can keep its interpolation (PCGAMGSetReuseInterpolation) and only recompute its coarse operators.

Faces between two valid cells on one level have two-point stencils, which fillMatrix computes as it goes. The other faces have stencils that are expensive to compute, so they are computed once per define and stored without beta and b; fillMatrix then only multiplies them by beta and the b-coefficient on the face. The stored stencils are over PETSc rows, with ghost cells already replaced, and are held per category so that fillMatrix iterates over each category on its own:

  • Domain faces: m_domainFluxStencils, with their boundary weights in m_boundaryWeights.
  • EB faces: m_ebFluxStencils.
  • Fine side of a refinement boundary: m_fineCFFluxStencils, multiplied by the fine-face b.
  • Coarse side of a refinement boundary: m_coarCFFluxStencils, one stencil per fine face under the coarse face. Each is multiplied by the b-coefficient on its fine face, which fillMatrix first copies from the fine level into the index space of the coarse level (m_bCoefFiCo).

Only the domain faces are handled so far, and their stencils are computed in fillMatrix rather than stored; the other categories, and the functions that define them, are placeholders.

◆ addBoundaryConditionsToRHS()

void PetscHelmholtzOp::addBoundaryConditionsToRHS ( Vec & a_rhs) const
virtualnoexcept

Move the boundary data part of the domain boundary fluxes into a right-hand side.

The operator is A*phi + c, where A is the matrix and c holds the w_B * g parts of the boundary fluxes, scaled like the rest of the face flux. Solving A*phi + c = rhs is the same as solving A*phi = rhs - c, so this subtracts c from a_rhs. It evaluates the boundary data but not the stencils, using the w_B kept from assembly. Only boundary terms are added: the domain boundary data now, and the EB boundary data and surface charge later. The caller supplies the volume source in a_rhs, kappa-weighted as described for the class.

[in,out]a_rhsRight-hand side, on the rows of the PETSc grid
