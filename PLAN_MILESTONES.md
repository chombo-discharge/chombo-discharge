# Cut-cell geometry — milestones 2 and 3

Milestone 1 (`PLAN.md`) replaces Chombo's moment machinery with integrals of an explicitly
stored polyhedron, behind `Driver.geometry_generation = polyhedral`, with no changes to Chombo.
It deliberately stops there: same grids, same graph, same classification, different moments — so
the solvers, robustness and accuracy can be exercised before anything structural moves.

These two milestones are what that unlocks. They are stated here so the Milestone 1 interfaces
are shaped for them, not so they are built now.

---

## Milestone 2 — refine parent cells

Take a cut cell generated by Milestone 1 and produce its eight children by clipping, so that
every coarsening relation is an identity about a partition rather than a condition to satisfy.
This is the operator already validated in the prototype: 230,400 descendants over nine
geometries, all seven moments conserving to 9.4e-16, zero topology defects.

### What changes from Milestone 1

**The surface must be retained.** Milestone 1 builds a `CutCellSurface` per cut cell, takes its
moments, and discards it. Milestone 2 keeps it, in a `LevelData<BaseIVFAB<CutCellSurface>>` held
on the chombo-discharge side.

It must be *retained*, not recomputed. `BrentRootFinder` runs up to `MAXITER = 100`
implicit-function evaluations per crossing, twelve crossings per cell, and
`m_implicitFunctionGas` walks every electrode and dielectric on every call — 10²–10³ evaluations
per cell against a 2.7 µs body build. Retention costs memory only: 23 reals per cut cell with
bends off, 46 with them on, roughly 37 MB per rank at 100k cut cells.

**The shop becomes stateful.** `fillGraph` is `const`, so the retained surfaces live beside the
shop rather than in it, or behind `mutable`. A detail, but it wants deciding before the
Milestone 1 interfaces harden.

### Where the children go

`EBIndexSpace` generates only its *finest* level from the `GeometryService`
(`EBISLevel.cpp:446`) and builds every coarser level by coarsening
(`EBIndexSpace.cpp:238`). So "refine a cut cell" means producing a level finer than the current
finest — a second `EBIndexSpace` in chombo-discharge, not a change to `EBISLevel`.

Asked to fill that finer level, the shop **clips the retained coarse bodies** rather than
re-evaluating the implicit function. Re-evaluating would give Chombo-like behaviour and lose
exact conservation against the parent; both paths are worth having, behind one flag, because the
difference between them is a direct measurement of what conservation is worth.

### What must be answered

- **The graph across the refinement boundary.** A fine cut cell abutting a coarse one needs
  apertures both sides agree on. The partition property gives this exactly — the four fine face
  apertures sum to the coarse one — but the EBGraph has to be told, and coarse-fine EB interfaces
  are the hardest part of EB AMR.
- **Single-valued children, by construction** (`PLAN.md` §4). Fluid arriving in several pieces is
  one VoF holding their sum, as `GeometryShop` already does. Already validated: 4 such cells in
  230,400, all conserving.
- **Depth.** Verified to depth 2 with the current representation; depth 3 was clean under the
  earlier one and has not been re-run since the polyline representation and the four-crossing
  decider fix landed. A ~20-minute run, worth doing before this milestone starts.
- **`a_B` (`PLAN.md` §7.1).** The one item needing a Chombo change. Not a blocker for Milestone
  1; it becomes one here if coarsened levels are expected to conserve `bndryCentroid` on curved
  geometry.

## Milestone 3 — AMR-aware geometry generation

Stop computing moments uniformly on the finest specified level. Instead generate on a hierarchy
where near-planar regions stay coarse and highly-curved regions refine, so resolution is spent
where the geometry actually needs it.

This is the milestone the whole design exists for. Milestones 1 and 2 are its preconditions:
Milestone 1 makes the moments integrals of a surface, and Milestone 2 makes refinement a
partition of that surface.

### The curvature indicator is already computed

The face-bend search (`PLAN.md` §3.1) traces the true contour and records how far it departs
from the straight chord between two crossings. The largest such offset, divided by `dx`, is a
dimensionless, per-cell measure of how badly a piecewise-linear surface represents the geometry
at this resolution. It is exactly the quantity that should drive refinement, and it falls out of
generation rather than needing a separate pass.

Measured, it already separates the cases cleanly: 0.00 bends per cell on an oblique plane,
0.23–0.26 on rotated polyhedra, ~7 on a sphere or torus. A planar facet asks for nothing; a
sphere asks everywhere; a polyhedron asks only along its edges.

### The sheet-collapse ratio is the second indicator, and it is sharper

The bend offset measures *curvature*. It does not fire on a sharp edge: a rotated cube needs
0.23 bends per cell, essentially none, because each face contour really is a straight chord — the
surface is flat right up to the edge and then turns. Yet those are exactly the cells where the
single-sheet collapse hurts most.

`a_B / a_B_true` catches them. It is 1.0 for a single planar sheet and falls as the patch folds
or the sheets oppose, and it needs no new data — it is the same pair of quantities Milestone 1
already carries for `a_B` (`PLAN.md` §7.1). On the worst cell of each geometry the collapse gap
`1 − a_B/a_B_true` runs 3.7e-16 on an oblique plane, 4.9e-3 on a sphere, and **4.6e-1 on a
rotated cube** — where the bend offset sees almost nothing.

So the two are complementary and both are free:

| indicator | fires on | blind to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bend offset / `dx` | curvature | sharp edges |
| `1 − a_B/a_B_true` | folds, disjoint sheets, edges | nothing that matters, but noisier on slivers |

Refine on the union. A third, more expensive diagnostic is the **aperture defect** — the gap
between our aperture and a one-level-refined coarsening of it — which measures directly what
refinement would buy, at the cost of a trial refinement.

### Some cells never separate, and the criterion must know it

`PLAN.md` §4.1 shows three regimes under refinement. A planar patch is already exact. A cell
with *disconnected* sheets resolves completely in one level once the pockets land in different
children — the axis-aligned simplex goes from `a_B = 0.040178` to the exact true area `0.120533`
in a single step. But a **convex edge stays inside a cell at every level**: the rotated cube
climbs 0.498767 → 0.609508 → 0.694278 against a true area of 0.927938 and keeps climbing.

That matters for the refinement criterion. Refining on the collapse ratio will keep tagging an
edge cell forever, because the ratio improves but never reaches 1. The criterion needs either a
floor on level, or a second test on whether the ratio is still *improving* per level, so that a
permanent compromise is recognised as permanent rather than chased.

That is not a reason to avoid the indicator; it is the one piece of the Milestone 3 criterion
that has to be designed rather than measured.

### Two ways to get the grid

**Tag during generation.** Generate coarse, evaluate the indicator, tag, refine the tagged cells
with Milestone 2's clipper, repeat. The hierarchy is discovered rather than specified, and
conservation holds by construction because every level below the first is a partition of the one
above.

**Accept a grid.** The caller passes an AMR hierarchy — for instance one already tagged for
solution features — and the EB is generated to match. Simpler, and it makes the EB grid and the
solver grid the same object, which is what `Realm` already wants.

These are not exclusive: the first produces a hierarchy, the second consumes one, and the useful
system probably does both, with the geometry tags unioned into the solver's.

### What must be answered

- **Does Chombo's EBIS tolerate a partial fine level?** `EBISLevel` carries a `DisjointBoxLayout`,
  so a fine level whose grids cover only the tagged region may work as-is. Whether
  `EBIndexSpace`, `EBISBox` and the stencil machinery behave at the resulting edge is the central
  unknown, and it should be answered before anything is built — cheaply, by defining such a level
  by hand and seeing what breaks.
- **Or does the EB belong on the AMR grids directly?** chombo-discharge's `Realm` already carries
  per-level grids and per-level `EBISLayout`s. Generating the EB onto *those* rather than onto a
  private uniform hierarchy is the more natural fit, and it may be less work than making
  `EBIndexSpace` accept partial levels.
- **What the solvers require at an EB coarse-fine interface.** This is the real risk in the whole
  programme. The geometry can be made exactly conservative across the interface; whether the
  existing multigrid, flux registers and redistribution stencils accept a cut cell whose
  neighbour is a coarser cut cell is a separate question, and it is not one the geometry work can
  answer on its own.
- **Load balance.** Refining only curved regions concentrates cut cells, and cut cells are the
  expensive ones. The existing tile-based load balancing will need geometry-aware weights.

### Why the order matters

Each milestone is independently useful and independently testable, which is the point of
splitting them:

| | delivers | provable on its own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | moments as integrals of an explicit surface, switchable | A/B on a Poisson solve, same grids |
| 2 | children that exactly partition their parent | every coarsening relation, to 1e-16 |
| 3 | resolution where the geometry needs it | cost at fixed accuracy, against a uniform fine run |

Milestone 1 can ship and be used without 2. Milestone 2 can ship and be used without 3. Nothing
in 3 requires revisiting a decision taken in 1.
